Finding a Balance with AI Agents in Software Engineering

Working at Venuiti has given me the chance to hear different perspectives on AI agents and the future of software engineering. A few conversations have stayed with me.

When I spoke with our CEO Thomas S. and Jordan H., we discussed what it takes to design a real agent loop: one that can work toward an outcome, evaluate its progress, and continue rather than simply produce a single answer. We also talked about the bigger picture. Human attention is limited, so the goal is to direct it toward the decisions that matter most.

A conversation with Artur S. brought another side of the problem into focus. AI does not always have the right context, especially in backend work, and can miss what is important. Someone still needs to understand the system, decide what matters, and review the result.

With Brent M. , I also discussed how a multi-level decision network could preserve context and maintain a traceable tree of decisions as information changes. One approach I proposed is ContextDB (https://zangjiucheng.github.io/ContextDB/), which explores how agents can share context and track when prior knowledge needs to be reconsidered.

For me, the balance is to give agents room to handle well-defined work while keeping people responsible for context, judgment, and outcomes. As these tools improve, I think software engineers will spend more attention on understanding systems, making decisions, and verifying that the result actually solves the problem.
